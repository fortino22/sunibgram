# How Your Feed Works

Your home feed isn't random. The **For You** tab is built just for you — it mixes
posts from people you follow, the communities you've joined, and what's buzzing
across campus right now. This page explains, in plain terms, how Sunibgram
decides what to show you and in what order.

---

## What goes into For You

Every post in your feed is given a quiet "interest score." A few things shape it:

- **How people are reacting to it** — likes, comments, reposts, bookmarks, and shares.
- **How fresh it is** — newer posts get a boost.
- **Who posted it** — your own posts, people you follow, your communities, and campus-wide trending posts are all blended together.

You never have to do anything. The feed updates as posts get more reactions and
as new ones arrive.

---

## Not all reactions count the same

A like is great, but some reactions say more about how much a post resonated.
When someone reposts, bookmarks, or comments, they're putting in more effort — so
those count for more than a plain like.

| Reaction   | How much it counts |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| Repost     | Counts the most    |
| Bookmark   | Counts a lot       |
| Comment    | Counts a lot       |
| Share      | Counts a bit more  |
| Like       | The baseline       |

Replies to other posts show up too, but they're nudged down a little compared to
original posts — your feed leans toward standalone posts over back-and-forth replies.

---

## Newer posts rise, older ones fade

Sunibgram is about what's happening *now*. A post is at its strongest right after
it's posted, then gently fades over the next several hours as it gets older. A
buzzing post can stay near the top for a while, but a quiet older post will slip
down to make room for fresher ones.

Posts older than about a week won't appear in For You at all — the feed keeps
itself recent.

---

## Fresh from the last few minutes

Brand-new posts (from roughly the last 15 minutes) get a reserved spot near the
top, newest first — even before they've had time to collect reactions. That way
you don't miss something that just dropped. Only a couple of these "fresh" posts
appear at a time so they don't crowd out everything else.

---

## You won't see the exact same feed twice

When several posts are about equally interesting, Sunibgram gently shuffles them
so your feed feels fresh each time you open it — instead of showing the identical
order over and over. Posts stay in roughly the right place by interest; the
shuffle just adds variety among similar ones.

Want a totally fresh mix? **Refresh** the feed and it reshuffles for you.

---

## Fair to everyone

No single account can take over your feed. Even if someone posts a lot, only a
handful of their posts can appear near the top, with a limit on how many show up
overall. This keeps your feed varied and gives more voices a chance to be seen.

---

## The "New posts" banner

While you're scrolling, Sunibgram quietly checks for new posts every few minutes.
When some arrive, a small **"N new posts"** banner appears at the top. Tap it and
the new posts slide in on top — your current spot stays put and nothing gets
reshuffled underneath you.

---

## Following and Communities tabs

For You is the smart, personalized feed. The other tabs are simpler on purpose:

- **Following** — posts from people you follow, in time order (newest first).
- **Communities** — posts from a community, with sort options like **Latest**, **Top**, and **Most liked** so you can browse the way you want.

Use For You to discover, and the other tabs when you want a straightforward,
predictable view.

---

## Quick questions

**Why am I seeing this post?**
Because it's getting good reactions, it's recent, and it comes from someone you
follow, a community you're in, or it's trending across campus.

**Why did the order change when I refreshed?**
Refreshing reshuffles posts of similar interest and pulls in the latest reactions,
so the mix feels fresh.

**Why didn't my friend's post show up?**
It might be set to private, it could be older than a week, or you may have blocked
or muted that account. Their posts will still appear normally on their profile and
in your Following tab.

**Do I need to set anything up?**
No. The feed learns from who you follow, the communities you join, and what's
popular — it adjusts on its own.
